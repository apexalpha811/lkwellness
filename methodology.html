The project is funded entirely with equity. There is no lender, no debt service and therefore no coverage ratio: every dollar of operating income accrues to the equity, and nothing is senior to it.

Unlevered yield on equity is stabilized annual operating income of $2,697,611 divided by the $4,750,000 raised. It is stated before tax, before any distribution policy, before maintenance capital and before any assumption about a sale, so it is not an investor return. No internal rate of return or exit multiple appears anywhere in this document, because nothing in this model supports a terminal value that could be sourced.

Cumulative cash across the modelled 36 months is $4,589,439. That figure includes the $540,000 of founding-member prepayments collected during construction, which is why the first year ends positive on a negative operating year. The equity is repaid out of that cash in month 37, which is past the end of the 36-month model and is therefore extrapolated at the stabilized run rate.

The working capital reserve of $1,105,800 is computed, not chosen: it is sized against a modelled cash trough of $42,467 at month 9.

The reserve exceeds the trough it covers by a wide margin, 26.0 times over. With no lender there is no mandated reserve and no covenant to cure, so the cushion is a deliberate choice: it funds a materially slower ramp than the one modelled without going back for a second raise. It is idle cash, and it is named here rather than left to be discovered.

The round: ownership, ratchet and exit

The raise is $4,750,000 for a starting stake of 30%, which ratchets toward a hard cap of 40% if the business underperforms. Every figure in this section is indicative and subject to definitive documents.

StakePre-moneyPost-moneyEntry multiple
Starting terms30%$11,083,333$15,833,3335.87x
Ratchet cap40%$7,125,000$11,875,0004.40x

Entry multiple is post-money against base-case stabilized annual operating income of $2,697,611.

The two endpoints are the same arithmetic seen twice: $4,750,000 divided by 30% is $15,833,333, and by 40% is $11,875,000. In valuation terms the ratchet is exactly a halving of the pre-money, $11,083,333 to $7,125,000.

Why 30% and not more. At 30% the round prices at 5.87x base-case operating income, inside the 5.0x to 6.5x band published for a single location under a strong operator with documented procedures. A 25% stake would have priced it at 7.04x, above every single-location band and inside the range for multi-location platforms, which is not a defensible ask for one building that does not exist yet.

The ratchet

Ownership is measured on trailing twelve-month operating income at stabilization. At or above $2,200,000 the investor holds 30%. Below it, ownership rises on a straight line to the 40% cap, which is reached at the low case of $1,244,500. The ratchet moves one way only: outperformance does not reduce the stake below 30%.

The ratchet cushions the downside without making the investor whole. At the low case 40% of $1,244,500 pays $497,800, still less than the $809,283 that 30% of base case pays. That is a property of the structure, not an error in it, and the brief says so on the slide rather than leaving it to be worked out.

Distributions

Two assumptions are shown because the follow-on rights and the cash yield pull against each other. Under full distribution the investor receives their share of all operating income. Under the expansion reserve, 25% of operating income is retained to fund the second location and the investor receives their share of the rest. Neither is a commitment to distribute, and both are stated before tax and before maintenance capital.

Exit

Three paths. Buyback: the company holds a right, not an obligation, to repurchase investor equity in a window in years 3 to 5, priced off trailing twelve-month operating income at a multiple agreed in the definitive documents. That multiple is deliberately not fixed here; where comparable multiples sit in year three is not knowable now. Right of first refusal: existing investors get first right to match any third-party offer before an outside sale proceeds. Strategic sale: the primary long-term path, and the reason the follow-on rights matter.
